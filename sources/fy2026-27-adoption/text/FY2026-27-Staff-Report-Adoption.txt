Executive Summary 
This report presents the information and actions necessary to adopt the FY 2026-27 
Operating Budget and the first year of the FY 2026-28 Biennial Capital Improvement 
Program (CIP) for the City of Santa Monica, the Santa Monica Housing Authority, and 
the Santa Monica Parking Authority. Staff also requests that Council approve the 
second year of the FY 2026-28 Biennial CIP Budget. Because the City Charter requires 
annual budget adoption, staff will return in June 2027 to present the second year of the 
CIP for formal adoption, including any necessary revisions.

Background 
Operating Budget 
The FY 2026-27 Operating Budget represents the second year of the FY 2025-27 
Biennial Budget adopted in June 2025, updated to incorporate the Realignment Plan 
and March 2026 operational refinements. At the May 26 study session, Council 
reviewed the revised FY 2026-27 proposed operating budget, including updated 
revenue estimates, departmental alignment adjustments, and personnel changes. 
 
Capital Improvement Program 
The FY 2026-28 Biennial CIP Budget also received extensive Council review in May. 
The CIP presented for adoption remains consistent with that version, with three targeted 
revisions: 
• Advancement of the Santa Monica Neighborhood Greenways Project from FY 
2027-28 
• Acceleration of the 4th Street Transit Priority Improvements 
• A reduction in FY 2027-28 funding for an AHSC-supported project  
Beyond these revisions, the CIP continues to reflect the City’s structural shift to relocate 
recurring maintenance, technology, and fleet replacement back into the operating 
budget. This allows the CIP to remain focused on true capital improvements, including 
park enhancements, major event readiness, mobility investments, and infrastructure 
renewal. 
 
Citywide Expenditure Budget Totals 
The final FY 2026-27 citywide budget for adoption totals $908.8 million, net of 
reimbursements and transfers, across all funds. The Citywide staffing plan includes 
2,287.6 full-time equivalent (FTE) positions across all funds, including 112.3 FTE 
temporary and 2,175.3 FTE permanent positions.

Response to Questions on Aquatics Fees 
At the May 26, 2026 budget study session, public comment was received 
regarding increases for adult aquatics permit fees and expressing concern about 
the cost recovery rate and comparability to other cities. Staff would like to 
address those concerns. 
 
Santa Monica Aquatics fees have not increased since 2021. The proposed 8–9% 
adjustment, ranging from a $0.50 increase for Santa Monica–based nonprofit 
youth groups (short course) to up to $8 for non–Santa Monica organizations 
(long course), aligns with current aquatics programs and other Recreation and 
Arts Department fees. Upon additional review with the fee study consultant, staff 
reallocated support costs based on actual pool usage and updated the cost 
recovery calculations, which reflect a lower overall cost recovery level for 
aquatics permit groups. The updated analysis separately evaluated youth and 
adult permit groups, confirming that adult permit groups have a higher cost 
recovery level (58%) than for youth groups (44%), which is consistent with City 
policy to provide greater subsidies for youth programs. Staff analysis finds these 
fee adjustments reasonable and unlikely to cause significant hardship or 
displacement of users. These updated calculations do not change the fee 
schedule presented in May 2026. Staff conducted additional research and found 
that only a few nearby cities offer swimming lane rentals and that even with the 
fee adjustments, Santa Monica’s lane rental rates remain below those of nearby 
agencies, including the Cities of Long Beach, Torrance, and El Segundo. 
Additionally, comments regarding Santa Monica College cost allocation, pool 
scheduling, and the Santa Monica High School Joint Use Agreement involve

• Citywide Fines Updates (Attachment F). The master fine resolution includes 
non-standard administrative citation fines used to enhance compliance with the 
Santa Monica Municipal Code. As part of the annual budget review process, staff 
evaluated existing fines to ensure they remain aligned with enforcement needs, 
community impacts, and current operational practices. This year’s updates 
include targeted adjustments in areas where fine amounts no longer reflect the 
cost of administering and enforcing applicable regulations, as well as the removal 
of fines that are no longer utilized. As a result of the comprehensive review of the 
fines, staff recommend revising three existing fines and establishing one new fine 
to address emerging operational needs, as described below. 
Revisions to Existing Fines 
o Staff recommends increasing three existing Code Enforcement fines to 
strengthen compliance. The fines for Construction Work Without Permit, 
Construction Work with Expired Permit, and Non-Compliance with an 
approved Means and Methods Plan would each increase to $1,000, as 
current fine amounts do not provide a sufficient compliance-driven deterrent. 
New Fines 
o The Community Development Department is proposing a new non-standard 
fine with a base penalty amount of $500 for violations of the Tenant 
Protection During Construction Administrative Regulations (SMMC 8.100.70) 
to support enforcement of regulations that protect tenants’ health, safety, and 
welfare. 
 
These changes are limited in scope and are intended to maintain consistency, 
support efficient administration, and strengthen the City’s ability to address 
specific code-related impacts.

Fiscal and Compliance Requirements 
These resolutions ensure the City remains in compliance with state law and funding 
requirements related to appropriations limits and transportation revenues. 
 
• Gann Appropriations Limit (Attachment J) - State law requires all state and 
local governments to adopt an annual spending limit, commonly known as the 
“Gann Limit.” This limit caps how much tax revenue can be used for public 
services. The cap is adjusted each year for changes in population, inflation, and 
shifts in responsibility between different levels of government. When tax 
revenues exceed this limit, the excess must be returned to taxpayers through 
rebates or future tax reductions.  
 
Each June, concurrent with the budget adoption, the City Council adopts a 
resolution establishing Santa Monica’s Gann limit for the upcoming fiscal year. 
For FY 2026-27, the proposed limit is determined by applying adjustment factors 
from the California Department of Finance to FY 2025-26’s limit.

The City’s FY 2026-27 appropriations subject to the Gann Limit, primarily in the 
General Fund, are projected to be $3,207,182,066 below the allowed 
maximum. Tax revenues counted under this limit include property, sales, 
transaction and use, transient occupancy, utility, parking facility, and business 
license taxes. These amounts are reduced by refunds, certain benefits 
payments, qualified capital projects, and court or federal mandates. 
 
• SB1 Gas Tax Revenues (Attachment K) - In April 2017, SB1, formally known as 
the Road Repair and Accountability Act (RMRA) of 2017, was signed into law. 
SB1 establishes the Road Maintenance and Rehabilitation Program (RMRP) to 
address deferred maintenance on the state highway and local street and road 
systems. Current fund estimates provided by the League of California Cities 
indicate that the City of Santa Monica will be eligible to receive approximately 
$2.6 million in SB1 funding in FY 2026-27, in addition to the City’s existing annual 
gas tax revenues of approximately $2.6 million. Staff recommends approving the 
attached resolution identifying the use of FY 2026-27 SB1 estimated allocation of 
$2.6 million for the Annual Paving and Sidewalk Repair Program capital 
improvement project, which was already included in the FY 2026-28 Proposed 
CIP Budget that was presented to Council on May 26.

Pier Corporation Governance Update 
As noted in the related Pier Corporation Services Agreement item (under Consent Items 
for June 23, 2026), increased event activity at the Pier has created a need to further 
clarify the respective roles of the City and the Pier Corporation. A subsequent services 
agreement update will formalize these roles, while preserving Pier Corp’s core function 
in supporting the visitor experience and community-based programming. Future 
agreement updates will also address related budget and funding implications as part of 
the broader Pier governance review anticipated for early winter. 
 
Council Discretionary Funds Updated Balance 
Each year, Council allocates discretionary funds to assist the community in special 
projects or support important but otherwise unfunded priorities. Historically, Council has 
supported community partners like the Business Improvement Districts, Santa Monica 
traditions such as the Fourth of July Parade, and grants that allow Santa Monica youth 
to participate in special events.  
 
On October 14, 2025, Council updated its Council Discretionary Funding Policy 
whereby allocations to long-standing community events, such as the Business 
Improvement Districts, Fourth of July Parade, Westside Coalition Martin Luther King Jr. 
Celebration, Santa Monica Pride, and Santa Monica High School Grad Night, would be 
funded through the City Manager’s Office budget.  Furthermore, additional funding was 
appropriated so that the Council discretionary fund budget would total $140,000, which 
would allow each Councilmember to direct allocations of up to $20,000. As of June 10, 
2026, Council has allocated $46,000, leaving a remaining balance of undesignated 
Council discretionary funds of approximately $94,000. 
 
Including unprogrammed FY 2025-26 discretionary funds ($94,000) and FY 2026-27 
discretionary funds ($140,000), the Council has access to $234,000 in discretionary 
funds in FY 2026-27.  A five-year history of the projects and programs supported by 
Council discretionary funds may be found in Attachment M.

Environmental Review 
The actions to adopt the FY 2026-27 Operating Budgets, establish the Gann 
Appropriations limit, and amend the City’s Fiscal Policies are exempt from CEQA 
pursuant to Section 15061(b)(3) of the CEQA Guidelines (common sense exemption), 
which states that CEQA does not apply, “where it can be seen with certainty that there 
is no possibility that the activity in question may have a significant effect on the 
environment.” Operational budget and fiscal policy are administrative and programmatic 
in nature. Therefore, it can be seen with certainty that the actions would not result in 
adverse physical impacts on the environment, and as such, is exempt from CEQA. 
 
The changes to Citywide Fees and Fines and parking rates are exempt from CEQA 
pursuant to Section 15273, which states that CEQA does not apply to the 
establishment, modification, structuring, restructuring, or approval of rates, tolls, fares, 
and other charges. The actions for budget adjustments and salary rates and 
reclassification of positions are exempt pursuant to Section 15324 (Regulations of 
Working Conditions), which exempt actions to regulate employee wages, hours, or 
working conditions. Additionally, these changes are exempt pursuant to Section 
15061(b)(3) of the CEQA Guidelines (common sense exemption), which applies when 
“it can be seen with certainty that there is no possibility that the activity in question may 
have a significant effect on the environment.”
