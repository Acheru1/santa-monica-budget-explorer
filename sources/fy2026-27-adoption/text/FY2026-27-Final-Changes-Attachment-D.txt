Project Description 
The Bergamot Connector project will complete the City’s Michigan Avenue Neighborhood 
Greenway (MANGo) Bergamot Connector segment (21st Street from Pennsylvania Ave to 
Michigan Ave; Michigan Ave from 21st Street to Bergamot Arts Center), improving 
connectivity for residents and visitors to Bergamot Station, and promoting alternative modes

grant funds awarded through Metro's MAT Cycle 2 funding program. 
Project Justification 
The project completes the Bergamot Connector segment of the MANGo Plan adopted by 
City Council 2014. The Neighborhood Greenway along Michigan Avenue was first envisioned 
by Pico Neighborhood residents seeking a safer, calmer, and more inviting corridor for 
pedestrians and bicyclists. Historically, the Pico Neighborhood has disproportionately faced 
environmental impacts due to commuter traffic and emissions from the I-10 freeway, which 
split the neighborhood when it was built.

Project Description 
The Project will construct a Class IV protected two-way bikeway along 14th Street between 
Washington Avenue and Pico Boulevard, upgrading the existing Class II bicycle lanes to 
provide a safer and more comfortable facility for people of all ages and abilities. The Project 
includes ADA curb ramp upgrades at 14th Street and Michigan Avenue, a key connection to 
the Michigan Avenue Neighborhood Greenway (MANGo), as well as improvements at 
several high-priority Vision Zero intersections, including Broadway, Santa Monica Boulevard, 
and Pico Boulevard, which have a history of traffic collisions. Additional improvements 
include roadway resurfacing, traffic signal modifications and upgrades, installation of street 
lighting in select segments, and targeted sidewalk repairs. Safety enhancements will address 
locations with documented traffic conflicts by improving visibility, shortening pedestrian 
crossing distances, reallocating street space to better accommodate bicyclists, and 
optimizing traffic signal operations for all modes of travel. 
 
Project Justification 
The Project will improve safety, accessibility, and multimodal connectivity while 
strengthening connections to key community destinations, including Santa Monica College, 
the 17th Street/SMC Station, Lincoln Middle School, Memorial Park, the Michigan Avenue 
Neighborhood Greenway (MANGo), and the Pico Neighborhood.

PROJECT DETAIL - Department of Transportation 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Project # 
C0948 
 
Target Completion Date 
FY 2027-28 
 
Total Project Budget 
$500,000 
 
 
 
Project Description 
This project will install transit priority improvements on 4th Street in Downtown Santa Monica, 
including a southbound curb running bus lane between Wilshire Boulevard and Colorado 
Boulevard and two northbound queue jumps at Broadway and Santa Monica Boulevard. The 
project includes signal timing analysis, traffic signal modifications, signage, striping, and 
related installation work to improve bus operations, travel efficiency, and transit reliability. 
 
Project Justification 
Approximately 400 buses operate daily in each direction along the 4th Street corridor 
between Wilshire Boulevard and Colorado Boulevard, making it one of the City’s busiest 
transit corridors. Implementing bus lanes, queue jumps, and related transit priority 
improvements will improve bus speed, reliability, and rider experience, helping increase 
transit ridership and supporting the City’s goal of activating economic opportunity and 
growth by moving residents, workers, and visitors through Downtown Santa Monica in an 
efficient and environmentally sustainable manner.

Project Description 
This project will design, construct, and evaluate quick build traffic safety improvements along 
the Michigan Avenue and Washington Avenue neighborhood greenways to support 
expansion of the City’s bicycle and active transportation network. Improvements will include 
traffic calming features such as diverters, chicanes, speed cushions, curb extensions, 
signage, and striping, along with community engagement, data collection, and project 
evaluation activities funded through a grant administered by Southern California Association 
of Governments (SCAG). 
Project Justification 
This project advances implementation of the Neighborhood Greenway network identified in 
the Bike Action Plan and MANGo Plan by addressing ongoing traffic safety and congestion 
concerns while improving bicycle and pedestrian connectivity. The project responds to 
strong community demand for expanded greenway infrastructure and will improve access 
and safety along key neighborhood routes connecting schools, parks, and residential areas, 
including Franklin Elementary School, Lincoln Middle School, St. Monica’s Catholic School, and 
Douglas Park.
